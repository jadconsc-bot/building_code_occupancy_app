### Scene 2: Understanding Occupancy Groups (0:45-3:00)

**VISUAL**: Animated diagram of occupancy groups, examples

**NARRATOR**:
"The NBC defines five occupancy groups:

**Group A - Assembly**: Buildings used for gathering of people. Examples include theatres, churches, restaurants, and bars. Assembly occupancies have strict egress and fire safety requirements.

**Group B - Institutional**: Buildings for care, treatment, or custody of people. Examples include hospitals, schools, and prisons. These have specialized life safety requirements.

**Group C - Residential**: Buildings for sleeping purposes. Examples include apartments, hotels, and dormitories. Residential buildings have specific egress and fire separation requirements.

**Group D - Office and Commercial**: Buildings for business, office, or retail use. Examples include office buildings and retail stores.

**Group E - Industrial**: Buildings for manufacturing or storage. Examples include factories, warehouses, and laboratories.

Each group has multiple divisions with more specific requirements. For example, Group A includes A-1 (theatres), A-2 (restaurants), A-3 (churches), and A-4 (arenas)."

### Scene 4: Voltage Drop Calculator (8:00-12:00)

**VISUAL**: Screen recording of Voltage Drop Calculator

**NARRATOR**:
"The Voltage Drop Calculator ensures that electrical conductors are properly sized to minimize voltage drop.

Voltage drop is the reduction in voltage that occurs as electricity travels through a conductor. If voltage drop is too high, equipment won't operate properly.

The NBC limits voltage drop to:
- 3% from the service entrance to the point of use
- 5% total from the service entrance to the branch circuit

Let's calculate voltage drop for a 150-foot feeder from the service entrance to a sub-panel.

Enter the circuit parameters:
- Conductor size: 2/0 AWG copper
- Conductor material: Copper (lower resistance than aluminum)
- Circuit length: 150 feet
- Phase type: Single-phase (for this example)
- Load current: 100 Amps

CodeComply calculates:
- Conductor resistance: 0.000098 ohms per foot
- Total resistance: 0.0147 ohms (150 feet × 0.000098)
- Voltage drop: 1.47 volts (100 Amps × 0.0147 ohms)
- Percentage drop: 0.61% (1.47V ÷ 240V)

Result: **PASS** - Voltage drop is well below the 3% limit.

If the voltage drop were too high, CodeComply would recommend a larger conductor size. Let's try with a smaller conductor to see what happens.

If we use 4 AWG copper instead:
- Voltage drop: 3.68 volts
- Percentage: 1.53%

Still compliant, but closer to the limit. For a 150-foot run, 2/0 AWG is the better choice."

### Scene 5: Conduit Fill Calculator (12:00-15:00)

**VISUAL**: Screen recording of Conduit Fill Calculator

**NARRATOR**:
"The Conduit Fill Calculator ensures that electrical conductors are installed in conduit with proper fill percentages.

The NBC limits conduit fill to prevent damage to conductor insulation and allow for heat dissipation:
- 1 conductor: 53% fill
- 2 conductors: 31% fill
- 3 or more conductors: 40% fill

Let's calculate the fill for a 1-inch EMT conduit with three conductors.

Select the conduit:
- Type: EMT (Electrical Metallic Tubing)
- Size: 1 inch

Add conductors:
- First conductor: 2/0 AWG THHN
- Second conductor: 2/0 AWG THHN
- Third conductor: 2/0 AWG THHN

CodeComply calculates:
- Conduit area: 0.864 square inches
- Conductor area (each): 0.1074 square inches
- Total conductor area: 0.3222 square inches
- Fill percentage: 37.3%

Result: **PASS** - Fill is within the 40% limit for 3+ conductors.

If we tried to add a fourth conductor:
- Total conductor area: 0.4296 square inches
- Fill percentage: 49.7%

Result: **FAIL** - Exceeds 40% limit. Recommendation: Use 1.25-inch conduit instead.

This tool helps us make sure our conduit sizing is correct before installation."
